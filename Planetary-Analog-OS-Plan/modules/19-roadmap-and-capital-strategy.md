# 19 — Roadmap and Capital Strategy

## 0–90 days after v0.1

### Product

- Add two additional morphology-study templates only after reviewing v0.1 findings.
- Improve source/product onboarding.
- Add study-package import/export.
- Add structured reviewer comments.

### Evidence

- Conduct five to ten interviews with planetary robotics, analog, geospatial, and field-study users.
- Seek one authorized low-impact study or simulation experiment.
- Record pre-study expectation and post-study result separately.
- Start negative-result and disagreement registry.

### Governance

- Create site-data sensitivity and publication policy.
- Draft community/site-steward partnership terms.
- Add role/status fields for local authority and access.

### Funding

- Submit a small research/public-interest grant only after a real pilot question is defined.
- Do not hire or add infrastructure before evidence of repeat use.

## 3–12 months

- Build H1 modular research platform.
- Support institutional private study packages and review workflows.
- Add PostGIS/object storage only when static packages become insufficient.
- Integrate field/simulation outcome capture.
- Establish an expert advisory group.
- Publish method paper or technical report if review supports it.
- Test paid pilots with a clear acceptance metric.

## Years 1–3

- Expand task families: terrain morphology, navigation-study design, sampling, excavation/ISRU, EVA route study where data/support justify it.
- Build multi-source catalog and standards mappings.
- Add simulator/lab integration.
- Establish governed site/study partner network.
- Create benchmark suite and external challenge.
- Add calibrated ML only after outcome corpus exists.
- Obtain security/accessibility/procurement posture needed for institutional buyers.

## Years 3–5

- Multi-agency federation and controlled data planes.
- Signed evidence packages and durable study identifiers.
- Outcome-based transfer analytics.
- Regional partners and community governance.
- Industry integrations with robotics/test planning systems.
- Public standards and interoperability adoption.

## Years 5–10

- Become a shared evidence layer for planetary analog programs if independent adoption emerges.
- Support long-lived research records and cross-agency comparisons.
- Establish an independent governance foundation or standards body if platform neutrality becomes important.
- Preserve open schemas and exportability.

## Funding ladder

| Stage | Funding target | Proof required |
|---|---|---|
| Prototype | Bootstrapped/competition/public research | Working evidence-bounded nucleus |
| Research kernel | Grant, university, pilot | Study package reuse and expert review |
| Institutional pilot | Contract/research funding | Buyer acceptance criterion and outcome |
| Platform | Seed/strategic/research contracts | Repeat usage, retention, governance, margin |
| Network | Public-private/agency consortia | Independent adoption and validated outcomes |
| Standard | Long-term institutional/public-good funding | Interoperability and legitimacy |

## Capital allocation principles

Spend first on:

- source/data stewardship;
- scientific review;
- outcome collection;
- governance and site relationships;
- reproducibility and interoperability;
- user workflow evidence.

Spend later on:

- large compute;
- broad global coverage;
- advanced ML;
- glossy 3D visualization;
- enterprise microservices;
- marketplace growth.

## Scale reality

A large valuation is an output of sustained adoption, margins, defensibility, and market timing—not the starting objective. A “trillion-dollar plan” is therefore written as a conditional infrastructure thesis with disconfirming gates, not a promised valuation.
