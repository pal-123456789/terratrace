01 — THE PROBLEM

Resemblance is easy.
Transfer is hard.

Finding a landscape that looks like Mars takes a search box. Designing a terrestrial test whose result legitimately transfers to a Mars decision takes measurable requirements, a shared spatial support, and an honest account of every gap.

02 — THE METHOD

One scale.
Five worlds.

Each area of interest is warped to its own local azimuthal-equidistant metric grid using the correct body — Mars on a 3,396,190 m sphere, the Moon, Mercury, Ceres, and Earth on WGS84 — so slope and relief are measured in true, comparable metres. Candidates are ranked by Pareto dominance across independent descriptor “lenses”, never a composite score; a tie is a valid result.

01 · INGEST

Real NASA + ESA data

Mars MOLA, Moon LOLA, Mercury MESSENGER, Ceres Dawn; Copernicus GLO-30 for Earth — no API key.

02 · REPROJECT

Per-body metric grid

The correct body radius, true metres — not a shared-degree hack.

03 · GATE

Refuses bad data

Water/sentinel masking, flat-tile blocking, and a comparability gate that rejects a cell whose footprints are not truly matched.

04 · DECIDE

Pareto, not a score

Six independent lenses; tie, indeterminate and blocked are first-class outcomes, not failures.

04 — THE FINDING · REAL DATA

The method caught
its own false analog.

It would have been easy to stop at the first answer. Instead we tested it with rules frozen before the data were seen — and it did not survive. So we measured why.

Act 1 · Exploratory screen

“Craters of the Moon, Idaho” led

Mars MOLA versus real Earth terrains at a common metric support. On the core roughness/relief axes a documented basalt field was the closest match — it looked like an analog the engine had re-identified on its own.

Act 2 · Pre-registered test (protocol v2)

…and it did not survive

Matched physical footprints, supports at or above MOLA’s effective resolution, held-out plains plus contrast terrains. The engine abstained for all seven references — and Craters was nearest for a cratered highland just as readily as for volcanic plains. Claim withdrawn.

Act 3 · Measured, not asserted (protocol v3)

The nearest analog tracks roughness, not volcanic origin

Two smoothness-matched non-volcanic controls, 60 km windows, fresh plains and highlands. A smooth non-volcanic terrain sat on the Pareto front for every confirmatory volcanic plain, while basaltic origin bought nothing — the basalts came forward only for the rough highlands.
