Protocol v3 · confirmatory panel · 2026-11-05

Within this panel and lens, the nearest Earth analog tracks roughness, not volcanic origin.

Three confirmatory Martian volcanic plains, two cratered highlands, seven Earth candidates, 60 km windows, supports at or above MOLA’s effective resolution. The frozen rules returned one clear answer — and it retires the last standing explanation of the analog claim the project had already withdrawn.

S · smoothness — SUPPORTED (3/3) V · volcanic specificity — NOT SUPPORTED (0/3) R · resolution robustness — SUPPORTED (no reversals · 1 destabilised · 3 membership-changed)
Scope of the confirmatory result. The S/V/R verdicts below use the core decision lens. TerraTrace ships five default lenses and an opt-in sixth, but these are alternative views of the same terrain — they are not independent scientific replications of this finding.
One caveat, stated first. v3 is pre-specified but not fully pre-registered: the decision rules and the config were fixed before any v3 data were seen and are unmodified since; the config's parameters are equally unchanged, with one post-run comment correction recorded and reconciled. The protocol document, however, was committed after the run. That is weaker than v2's commit-before-run and is not presented as equivalent.

4 · The mechanism, visible in the cells

Smooth → smooth

Arcadia (σ 7 m) and Cerberus (σ 5 m) are matched by the two smoothest Earth candidates: Nullarbor (σ 5 m, limestone) and Llano Estacado (σ 14 m, sedimentary).

Rough → rough

Terra Sabaea (σ 148 m) and Terra Sirenum (σ 256 m) are matched by Harrat Khaybar (σ 102 m) — a basaltic field. The basalts win where the terrain is rough, not where it is volcanic.

And the one smooth basalt behaves as a smooth candidate: Deccan Traps (σ 32 m, flood basalt) enters layer 1 for Solis Planum — the roughest of the three plains references — precisely when the window is large enough to average its relief. It competes because it is comparatively smooth, not because it is volcanic. Within this panel and lens, origin does no work that roughness does not already do.

7 · Reported, never used as evidence

  • Adequacy @1500 m. For every reference some candidate falls within that reference's own internal variability — and Arcadia and Cerberus admit only the two smooth controls as adequate, the same two that carry S.
  • Adversarial stress on the primary cell (mars_arcadia @1500 m): verdict fragile — stable in 0.86 of 21 bounded perturbations, reversing under a 6-pixel crop-origin shift or when either smooth control is dropped. Reported, not hidden: the class-level S pattern is robust, but the single top-of-shortlist pick is a near-tie among similarly smooth terrains and should not be over-read.
  • Warnings: none. No honest support needed demotion or diagnostic reclassification.

8 · What this does — and does not — say

Does say

With MOLA + Copernicus GLO-30, real 60 km windows, honest supports and matched footprints, the Earth terrain that best matches a Martian volcanic plain is a smooth one — and at least as likely to be non-volcanic. The withdrawn Craters-of-the-Moon preference is explained by smoothness, and the explanation is now tested rather than asserted.

Does not say

That no Earth terrain resembles a Martian plain — the morphometric similarity is real and measured. That basalts are irrelevant to Mars analog work — they matter where the target is rough. Or anything about traversability, geologic equivalence, site access, or mission readiness.

9 · Limits, unchanged and load-bearing

  • One DEM source per body (MOLA, COP30), so references × supports are multiple comparisons, not independent replications.
  • COP30 is a surface model: it includes vegetation and buildings.
  • Each 60 km window samples a small part of a named unit.
  • The 3× effective-resolution figure is a heuristic, not a measured MOLA transfer function.
  • Candidate classes are literature assignments made before the run.

Falsifier for the next iteration: a pre-registered panel in which a smooth specifically volcanic terrain beats the smooth non-volcanic controls for Martian volcanic plains but not for highlands. Deccan Traps is the closest case here and it does not do that.

10 · Reproduce it — no API key, no account

# the Earth candidates come from the public ESA Copernicus GLO-30 COG mosaic on AWS
python scripts/fetch_earth_candidates.py --config config/matched_footprint_v3.yml --source cog
python scripts/run_matched_benchmark.py --config config/matched_footprint_v3.yml --out artifacts/mf_panel_v3

The COG path is verified bit-identical to the OpenTopography COP30 product (maximum absolute difference 0.0 m). The Mars side is the single global MOLA 463 m GeoTIFF. Both need rasterio.
