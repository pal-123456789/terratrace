D · Is the frontier robust to the axis set?

The lunar panel decides a winner by Pareto dominance over three declared axes. This section asks whether that winner is a property of the terrain or of the rule — and answers it against a control, because a Pareto frontier over eight units in three dimensions is expected to be fragile when a fourth coordinate arrives, any fourth coordinate.

measurementvaluewhat it means
candidate axes decision-tested141every admissible scalar in the engine’s own descriptor bank was added as a fourth axis in turn and the layer-1 set recomputed at all 18 cells
cells whose winner set changed0 to 18 of 1842 of the 141 axes changed nothing at all; adding a coordinate can only ever promote a co-winner, never remove one
an arbitrary coordinate15.90 of 18 cells on averageover 300 random draws, median 18, leaving the frontier intact in 12% of them — this is the baseline the numbers above must beat
axes beating that control0none. Every change above is what adding a dimension does, not evidence about a candidate
most independent descriptor/variogram/nugget, adjusted R² -0.511the three declared axes predict it no better than three random numbers would (chance level R² 0.4286) — and it changes the decision at 0 of 18 cells, which is how independence and decision-relevance come apart
what the axes cannot see48 of 180 bank scalarsthe three declared axes are exactly blind to a quarter-turn rotation of a planar surface (verified to 1e-13); the scalars listed here are the ones that are not, and they are the only ones this symmetry argument can even point at
What this bounds. The sole-non-dominated property is a property of running a Pareto rule over eight units in three dimensions, so it must not be read as evidence about candidates. The ranking stands as published — relative and provisional — and this measures exactly how provisional. It does not promote any Earth candidate: adding dimensions promotes co-winners without discriminating between them. EXPLORATORY SENSITIVITY PROBE.
